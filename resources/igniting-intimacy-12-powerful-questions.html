Igniting Intimacy: 12 Powerful Questions to Transform Your Relationship

5 minute read

Intimacy is often assumed to mean physical closeness, but it runs deeper than that. It is the sense of closeness and connection built in the daily moments between two people, not only in grand gestures.

Relationships can settle into routine: the same conversations, the same evenings, the same responses, until the flavour and spice start to fade. Curiosity and playfulness are often the first things to go, and they are exactly what can restore intimacy, without it needing to centre on sex.

The twelve questions below are designed to build that closeness and understanding at a deeper level. They work whether answered as a couple right now, or used individually as a way of getting clearer on what a relationship, current or future, could look like.

How to use these questions

Answer as though exploring the relationship right now, responding the way a partner would. It can also help to imagine how an ideal partner might respond, since that builds a framework for a future relationship. Anyone, whether single or coupled up, can use the questions to build a deeper understanding of themselves.

The 12 questions

  1. What's something you would love us to do more of if we had more time?
  2. What's something in our daily life that you find beautiful?
  3. What are three strengths you see between us that we possess as a team?
  4. What's something I do that always makes you smile?
  5. What's one crazy thing you'd like us to do that we've not done yet?
  6. How do I make you feel especially loved in our relationship?
  7. What are three qualities you admire about yourself?
  8. What's one of the biggest lessons you've learned in our relationship?
  9. What's one thing you will never do again in our relationship?
  10. What would you love for us to have accomplished five years from now?
  11. If we could share a superpower, what would it be?
  12. If you could invent a smoothie named after us, what would it be called, and what ingredients would go into it?

It is worth exploring the answers more deeply rather than moving straight to the next question. Staying curious and asking a follow-up, such as "What makes that important to you?" or "How does that make you feel?", turns a list of questions into a real conversation.

The important thing is to listen to these responses without judgement or criticism. Each answer belongs to the person giving it: their view, their feelings, their perceptions and their experience. Sitting back, listening fully and staying present while a partner answers is as much a part of this exercise as the questions themselves.

A bonus technique: L-RUGI

Alongside the twelve questions, a simple daily practice can do a lot to keep intimacy alive: five elements known as the L-RUGI technique.

L-RUGI, in brief

L
Love
R
Respect
U
Uplift
G
Gratitude
I
Inspire

This works best as a daily habit, ideally shared in person or over a call, since tone and eye contact carry as much meaning as the words themselves. Where speaking face to face isn't possible, a written message still carries real value.

Love

The expression of love goes beyond the usual "I love you." It is about naming the deeper emotions and connection shared with a partner: what makes your heart swell with affection, whether that's the way they make you laugh during tough times, their support in your endeavours, or the comfort of being truly understood. Love, in its purest form, is an acknowledgment of the unique qualities that make a partner an irreplaceable part of your life.

Respect

Respect means acknowledging and appreciating a partner for who they are: their individuality, their choices and their boundaries. Which specific qualities, actions or characteristics evoke admiration? What do you find honourable and valuable about them? When respect becomes a daily practice, it builds a foundation of trust and appreciation that allows the relationship to thrive.

Uplift

Feeling uplifted is not just about feeling good. It is about recognising the ways a partner lifts you up, emotionally, mentally or otherwise: the encouragement offered during challenging moments, the support for your goals, or the positive influence on your mindset. When both partners feel uplifted by each other, it creates a dynamic where each can flourish and bring out the best in the other.

Gratitude

It is easy to take things for granted. Gratitude is about naming specific actions, qualities or small moments that make the relationship special: the way they make your favourite meal after a tough day, their ability to bring calm to a chaotic situation, or the small gestures that show they were thinking of you. Gratitude turns routine moments into cherished memories and deepens connection.

Inspire

What is inspiring about a partner? It might be their passion, the resilience they show in the face of challenges, or the way they embrace life with enthusiasm. Sharing this adds a layer of excitement and shared purpose to the relationship. Naming the "why" behind each thing that's loved, respected, uplifting, appreciated or inspiring, rather than stopping at the observation itself, adds real depth to the exercise.

Used as a daily ritual, the L-RUGI technique and the twelve questions above work together: practical tools for building an emotional connection that is not only fulfilling but resilient. Consistency, more than any single conversation, is what deepens understanding and appreciation over time.
