7 Day ‘Me Time’ Plan

7 minute read

Whether a relationship is healthy or on shakier ground, and whether someone is single or looking for love, a strong relationship with yourself sets the bar for every other relationship in life. But daily life is busy. Work, home and a never-ending to-do list can make it hard to find time for anything else, let alone time for yourself, and that can feel even harder when used to putting other people first.

You can only be the best for others, when you are at your best.

The instruction airline crews give before every flight, to fit your own oxygen mask before helping anyone else with theirs, is a useful metaphor here: running low on your own reserves eventually leaves nothing left to help anyone else with. Without regular ‘me time’, burnout, stress, fatigue, reduced focus, anxiety, frustration and poor sleep can all follow. Letting go of the guilt and rewarding yourself with time that is genuinely your own is what keeps that from happening.

So what is ‘me time’?

It means different things to different people. ‘Me time’ is anything that lights you up: a walk, a trip to the cinema, a night out with friends, a massage, volunteering, or simply time spent losing yourself in something enjoyable. Building it in daily, even starting with ten minutes, matters more than any single longer break. The days below can be reordered from week to week; what matters is consistency, not rigid sequencing.

If we keep going we never have a chance to stop and appreciate what we've got, and ask what do I need for me right now?

— Naomi Martell-Bundock, wellbeing coach

Day 1

Purification (cleansing)

Dedicate the day to purification of body and mind: a day to cleanse, reflect and renew, and to listen to that intuitive inner voice. Consider:

  • Eating food that feels fulfilling, not depriving. Oils, fibre and juices are good places to start.
  • Using mind-body techniques such as meditation, breathing or yoga to free the mind and relax the body.
  • Spending time in nature, or tending to plants or a garden.
  • Unplugging for the day: no emails, no social media, the phone put away. It might not feel easy at first, but it becomes more comfortable with practice, and can be a chance to appreciate what those things add to daily life.
Day 2

Life plan

Round up what has happened during the week and celebrate the wins. This is also a good moment to revisit relationship goals, dreams and desires. Consider:

  • Are the goals, dreams and desires still clear? If they've changed, or new ones have appeared, update them.
  • Has any inspired action been taken?
  • Has any progress been made, or opportunities noticed, toward those goals?
  • Have any goals, dreams or desires already been achieved?
  • Have any signs or synchronicities stood out?
  • What acts of kindness have been given to others, received from others, and given to yourself?
Day 3

Get in touch with your body

Put real, loving time into taking care of your body today. Close your eyes and get curious: what does your body actually feel like? Send love to the parts you're not so keen on, and practise placing your hands on those areas without judgement.

  • A long, relaxing bath, whether as a way of letting go of the day or a refreshing start to it.
  • Pampering skin properly rather than rushing it: a face mask, a facial, or slowly massaging in lotion.
  • Looking at your full body in the mirror and speaking to yourself kindly, finding words to compliment your legs, arms, hair, eyes and mid-section.
  • Tending to your nails and, separately, your hair. Does the current style still flatter your face and colouring?
  • Paying attention to your feet, or kicking off your shoes and walking barefoot to feel grounded.
  • Dressing in something that feels genuinely expressive today, and saying, “I deserve to wear beautiful things because I love me!”
  • A massage, if tension needs releasing.
  • Sleeping in your most natural state tonight, if that feels comfortable: an underrated sign of self-acceptance.
Day 4

Staying connected: giving and receiving

Busy lives can quietly get in the way of keeping up with the people who matter, but connectedness is good for well-being. Consider:

  • Calling someone today. A phone conversation carries a depth that a message often can't.
  • Sending a text if a call isn't possible, as a small instant message of happiness.
  • Catching up with people through social networks.
  • A hug, or a gentler touch such as a hand on the shoulder or a soothing rub of the back.
  • Arranging a dinner, meeting up with friends, attending a social meet-up, or volunteering: all genuine opportunities for meaningful interaction.

A note on touch: keep any physical touch to people who you know will welcome it.

Day 5

Move your body

Do a physical activity that's genuinely enjoyed today, approached playfully rather than as a perfectionist. Trying something unfamiliar, a runner trying belly dancing, a yogi trying kickboxing, can be part of the fun. A few ideas:

  • Zumba, for anyone who likes to dance: an aerobic workout blending salsa, merengue, flamenco and other styles.
  • Rope jumping, a childhood favourite that also happens to be a full-body cardiovascular workout.
  • Hula hooping, a cardio workout that tones the waist, hips, buttocks, thighs, abdominals and lower back.
  • Pole dancing, a step outside the comfort zone for many, and a way to build both fitness and confidence.
  • Teaming up with a local league or with friends and family for a sport enjoyed in childhood, since the social side of a team makes it feel far less like exercise.
Day 6

Model excellence

Spend time today thinking about someone whose standard of excellence feels worth modelling. Consider:

  • Why do they inspire you?
  • What is their vision for their life, and how do they pursue it wholeheartedly?
  • What actions have they taken, in the past and now, to achieve their goals and dreams?
  • What actions have put them at the top of their game, and made a genuine difference?
  • Are they still holding on to their integrity?
  • How do they live in a way that's consistent with their highest values and deepest convictions?

This isn't about imitating their particular passion, goal or dream: following your own passions and staying original matters more. It's about understanding the steps they took, and building your own version of that.

Day 7

Creativity

The brain benefits from a bit of tension in the same way a muscle does. Spend time today giving the mind a creative workout. A few ideas:

Write poetry

Write a poem about the day, perhaps in the style of a favourite poet, or following a classic structure like a sonnet or haiku. Working within a clear structure pushes toward finding the right word or phrase, which can improve writing more broadly.

Read

Read a book or article unrelated to work or the usual reading list, to pick up something new. Learning about a different discipline can introduce new ways of thinking and behaving.

Personal art projects

A personal art project can fuel creativity, even if finding the right one takes a bit of searching online. Popular, low-pressure starting points include ‘painting by numbers’ and adult colouring books.

Together, these seven days offer a structure for building a week that includes real personal time, not just the leftover minutes at the end of a busy day. The days can be reordered, adapted or repeated; what matters most is coming back to them consistently.
